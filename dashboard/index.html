Which titles are earning their place?

Four public sources — two Netflix files that disagree with each other, IMDb, and Wikipedia — resolved to one model. Everything below is filterable by period, genre and type, which is the point: it replaces four spreadsheets nobody reconciles by hand.

Titles, ranked

Headline metric: reported hours per week available since release, with Top 10 longevity beside it. Titles need eight weeks available to be ranked — a title released days before a report closes would otherwise divide half a year of viewing by one week.

Genres that are watched more than they chart

Share of reported hours divided by share of Top 10 slots. Above 1 means a genre gets watched more than its chart presence suggests. The chart is capped at ten slots per category per week and hours are not, which is what makes the comparison say something. Bars show 95% bootstrap intervals over titles; genres whose interval clears 1 are the real ones.

Does a better rating hold the chart longer?

No. Raw correlation looks positive, but with size held constant the rating term is indistinguishable from zero. Weeks of chart time per unit, 95% intervals.

Does outside interest arrive first?

No — it arrives the same week. Best-fitting lag per title, negative meaning Wikipedia moved first.

Sources. Netflix engagement reports ("What We Watched", six half-years to June 2026) and the Netflix Global Top 10 weekly file, both published by Netflix; IMDb non-commercial datasets; English Wikipedia pageviews via the Wikimedia API, reached from IMDb ids through Wikidata.

What this cannot tell you. Hours are half-yearly, rounded to 100,000, and one platform only — nothing here is a market share. Genre comes from IMDb, not Netflix, and a title with three genres counts in all three. Wikipedia pageviews are English-language, so they under-represent non-English titles, which are half the Top 10 by construction. Titles under reported hours are left out of the table.

Per-title IMDb ratings and vote counts are deliberately not published here — IMDb's datasets are licensed for personal, non-commercial use, so this page carries the genre labels the analysis needs and the model coefficients, which are aggregates.
